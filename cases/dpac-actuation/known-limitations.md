# Known limitations — IC-DPAC-ACTUATION-001

This experiment is intentionally bounded.

- The initial reference evaluators are semantic/composition models, not production agent harnesses, policy engines, authorization servers, capability systems, or actuators.
- The initial DPAC model represents authority as observable boolean state sufficient to exercise the invariant. The GovOps delegated-loan pressure test adds monetary scope, exact target/amount binding, revocation-at-actuation, policy enforcement, capability revision, and single-use actuation state, but still does not prescribe an authority token, credential format, issuer, transport, or cryptographic mechanism.
- The enforced-boundary tranche adds concrete Docker network, process-principal, read-only filesystem, capability-drop, no-new-privileges, secret-mount, policy-mount, and actuator-journal evidence. It demonstrates more than `capability_controller_separate: true`, but it is still one bounded runtime realization rather than a general proof of non-collapsibility.
- Workflow/helper and actuator have no shared Docker network in the tested topology; Workspace is the only bridge. The Workflow/helper containers do not receive the runtime capability-policy mount or actuator credential. This does not prove resistance to compromise of the Docker host, daemon, runner, image supply chain, kernel, or other infrastructure outside the modeled security domains.
- The capability policy is independently supplied to Workspace as a read-only bind mount and is revision/digest-visible. The experiment does not model a production capability-administration control plane, multi-party administration, hardware root of trust, cloud IAM, Kubernetes admission, or remote policy authority.
- `DPAC-005` and the helper attack provide bounded direct/transitive non-collapse evidence, but the Lab still does not implement a general static or runtime dependency/control-graph analyser. Unknown transitive administrative paths remain outside the claim.
- The actuator uses an experiment-scoped shared secret available only to Workspace and actuator containers, plus disjoint networks. This is not a normative credential mechanism and does not establish production credential protection.
- Authority authenticity remains abstracted as supplied authority evidence. Workspace checks currentness, monetary scope, exact target/amount binding, and replay state, but this tranche does not cryptographically resolve delegation/authority provenance from GAAM, TEA, GovOps, TIS, or another upstream authority source.
- The actuator-owned journal makes no-effect claims stronger than relying on Workflow/Workspace denial responses, but it is an experiment-local journal rather than independently operated external telemetry or certification evidence.
- The composed pressure tests cover authority/capability scope divergence, revocation, capability-state TOCTOU, target substitution, amount widening, duplicate/retry execution, direct actuator bypass, policy mutation/admin attempts, a bounded helper path, and indeterminate capability state. They do not claim exhaustive coverage of prompt injection, confused-deputy behavior, side channels, kernel/container escape, credential theft, concurrency races, supply-chain compromise, or host compromise.
- The evidence remains self-authored and reproduced within the Lab. It is not independent implementation evidence, external review, certification, or an interoperability-tested claim.
- No normative change is made to TEA, GAAM, GovOps, TSMM, TIS, Trust Tasks, or any other upstream project. If implementation pressure exposes a reusable semantic gap, that gap must be raised to the owning layer separately.
- External adversarial harness integration remains deliberately deferred. The Agent Security Harness or another upstream tool may later be consumed as an external pressure-test input without becoming authoritative over Lab maturity claims.

Accordingly, passing runs establish that the repository-owned reference models preserve their declared DPAC boundaries for the recorded scenarios, and that the tested container topology enforces the specifically observed Workflow/Workspace/actuator separations. They do not establish production-grade or universally non-collapsible actuation control.
